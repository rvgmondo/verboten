# The Verboten voice

One person, two rooms. The website is the host at the door; social is the same
person behind the bar, pouring. Same character, same values, a little quieter
on the site because people are reading, not chatting.

**In one line:** confident and dry about the drink, warm about the people,
Afrikaans and English the way Pretoria actually speaks them. "Pure Mischief"
shows up as wit, not attitude.

Agreed with the owner, 18 September 2026. Everything written for the site,
emails and social follows this. The hard rules at the bottom are not style
preferences: some of them are law or the industry code.

---

## 1. About the drink: confident, dry, short

Say what it is and stop. Facts carry it, not adjectives. The house does not
need to tell anyone it is premium; it tells them what is in the glass.

- Yes: "Three years in oak. French casks. That is the whole trick."
- Yes: "Cold glass. Good ice. Do it properly."
- No: "An exquisite, elevated brandy experience crafted for the discerning."

## 2. About people: warm, inviting, generous

This is where the old site was too cold. When the copy turns to the person
reading, it opens the door: it invites, it welcomes, it tells you where to
find the house and when. It never pushes.

- Yes: "Come and find us. We will be the ones pouring."
- Yes: "Tell us the date and the crowd. We bring the bar."
- No: "Don't miss out! Order now before stock runs out!"

## 3. Afrikaans: the way you actually speak

Afrikaans turns up where it would in real speech: a greeting, an invitation,
a thank you, a sign-off, the house lines. On the website it is a warm aside,
never a whole paragraph, and the sentence around it always makes the meaning
clear to someone who does not speak it. (Social can go further, and often
posts wholly in Afrikaans.)

Use these as written. Anything not on this list, check with a fluent speaker
before it ships: bad Afrikaans is worse than none.

## 4. Mischief is wit, not attitude

One good turn of phrase per section is plenty. The wit is about the drink,
the rules and the name, never at the reader's expense, and never about how
much anyone drinks.

- Yes: "Some rules are meant to be questioned. The age rule is not one of them."
- Yes: "Brandy and Coke, the national serve. Done properly for once."
- No: anything that sounds like a dare, a frat party, or rebellion for its own sake.

## 5. Occasion first

People drink Verboten at a braai, with the rugby on, at a wedding, at the
market on a Saturday. (Say "the rugby", not "the Bokke": team names and
nicknames belong to their sponsors, and an alcohol brand borrowing one
invites an objection.) Put the drink where it lives. Say where and when, practically,
the way the Facebook posts do: what, where, when, and what to bring.

Never say or imply that drinking makes the occasion, the friends or the night.

## 6. What Verboten never sounds like

Corporate. Luxury-speak ("indulge", "exquisite", "elevate", "curated",
"journey", "experience" as a noun for a drink). A sales guru ("don't miss
out", countdown pressure, stacked exclamation marks). A frat party.
Motivational quotes. A technical brochure. A brand that needs approval.

## Hard rules (not negotiable)

**Copy rules**
- Banned everywhere: craft, crafted, craftsmanship, artisanal, handcrafted,
  boutique, micro, small-batch as identity, "legally produced" or anything
  defensive about legitimacy, lockdown, stacked empty superlatives.
- No em dashes, en dashes, ellipses or middots. Commas, full stops, restructure.
- No batch numbers and no "limited edition" framing.
- "VIR DIÉ WAT WEET" and "MEMORIES NOT REGRETS" spelled exactly, as designed moments.

**Facts**
- Never invent: no venues, stockists, dates, awards, people, processes,
  ingredients or history that the owner has not confirmed. If a line needs a
  fact nobody has given you, write around it.
- Which legal class the brandy belongs to is unconfirmed. Do not add any new
  claim about how it is made or how long every drop was aged. Existing product
  facts stay as the owner supplied them.

**The alcohol industry code (DF-SA 2026) and the law**
- The responsibility line is the code's exact wording, from
  `src/lib/compliance.ts`. Never paraphrase or restyle it.
- No alcohol strength in anything promotional: headlines, descriptions,
  social, emails. The product spec row is the only place it lives.
- Nothing that encourages drinking fast or drinking a lot, and no health,
  social, sexual or success claims.
- Nothing aimed at or appealing to under 18s. Paid creators must reasonably
  appear to be at least 25 (code 2.5.3.1); keep everyone in imagery clearly
  adult.
- Never put the drink near driving, cars in motion, or machinery. "Don't
  Drink and Drive." is one of the authorised messages.
- Competitions say under 18s cannot enter.

**Search**
- Page titles keep their search terms. Rewrite descriptions freely, but keep
  the words people search for and stay under about 155 characters.
