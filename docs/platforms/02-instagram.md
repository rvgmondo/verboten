# Verboten Spirits: Instagram Pack

One month of content a solo founder can actually run, in the house voice.

Reality this pack is built on: the account is starting from near zero, there is no analytics installed, and the only photography that exists is three studio product shots on black plus a set of real event photographs. So the calendar leans on what exists, uses text-on-black where a photo would be faked, and asks for exactly nine new shots, all of which can be taken on a phone in two sittings.

## 1. Profile

### Bio, three options (150 character limit)

**Option A, recommended. 136 characters.**

```
Pure Spirit. Pure Mischief.
South African brandy. 3 years in oak, finished in French oak.
Silverton, Pretoria. 18+
Delivery countrywide.
```

**Option B, story first. 138 characters.**

```
Pure Spirit. Pure Mischief.
Brandy from Silverton, Pretoria. Nothing leaves the house unless it earns the label.
18+ Delivered nationwide.
```

**Option C, insider first. 126 characters.**

```
VIR DIÉ WAT WEET
Pure Spirit. Pure Mischief.
South African brandy, 43%, aged in oak.
18+ only. R150 delivery, free over R2500.
```

Counts include the line breaks, which Instagram charges you for. Paste and check in the app before you save, because counters disagree about accented characters.

**Take Option A.** B is the better line but it only lands if you already know the brand, and nobody does yet. C spends the first line on a language most of your future followers do not read, which is the right move on a post and the wrong move on a bio. A tells a stranger what it is, where it is from, and that it will arrive at their door, without dropping the tagline. Save the Afrikaans for posts, where it is a moment instead of a barrier.

### Name field

```
Verboten Spirits | SA Brandy
```

28 characters. The field takes 30, so check as you type if you change the wording. Instagram search leans on the name and username fields, and bio text does far less for you there, which is why the category words have to sit here. Somebody typing "south african brandy" into search should be able to land on you. "Verboten" alone means nothing to a stranger and competes with a German adjective.

### Link strategy

Instagram lets you stack more than one link in the bio, five slots on most accounts. Open Edit profile to see what yours offers, use every slot you get, point them at your own domain, and skip the third-party link pages. A Linktree hands your only measurable traffic to somebody else's analytics.

| Slot | Link | Label |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The shop page on verboten.co.za | Shop |
| 2 | Premium Brandy product page | The Brandy, R450 |
| 3 | Two-bottle set product page | Two Bottles, R850 |
| 4 | https://wa.me/27753873456 | Order on WhatsApp |
| 5 | The story or about page | The House |

Two rules. Slot 1 is the one most people tap, so it changes when a campaign runs and otherwise stays on the shop. And append `?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=bio` to every site link today, even though nothing is measuring yet, so the day analytics goes in you have history instead of a blank page.

Separate job, same week: install analytics. Plausible or GA4, an hour of work. Right now you cannot tell the difference between a post that worked and a post that did nothing, which means month two is guesswork.

### Story highlights

Six covers, all the same: black tile, the logo centred, nothing else. The names carry the meaning.

1. **The Liquid.** Tasting notes, the pour, the specs, the serve.
2. **VIR DIÉ WAT WEET.** The knowledge posts. How to drink it, what oak does, the myths.
3. **Where To Find Us.** Bars, restaurants, markets, events.
4. **The House.** Silverton, 2020, the rule about the label.
5. **Orders.** Price, delivery, dispatch time, WhatsApp. The one people actually check before buying.
6. **Memories.** Event photographs, tagged posts, the nights that went well. Optional, start it once you have eight or more frames worth keeping.

### Age restriction, exact path

Non-negotiable for an alcohol account. The setting only exists on a professional account, so switch first if you have not.

**Switch to a professional account (skip if already done):**
Profile → menu (three lines, top right) → **Settings and privacy** → **Account type and tools** → **Switch to professional account** → choose **Business** → the alcoholic beverage category if it is offered, otherwise **Brand**.

**Set the minimum age:**
Profile → menu (three lines, top right) → **Settings and privacy** → **Business tools and controls** (it reads **Creator tools and controls** on a creator account) → **Minimum age** → **Default minimum age** → select **18** → **Done** on iOS, or the checkmark on Android.

The same screen lets you add country-specific ages that override the default. You ship South Africa only, so a global default of 18 is enough. Leave the country list empty.

Instagram moves these menus with app updates. If the labels above do not match what you see, search "minimum age" in the Instagram Help Centre from inside the app and follow the current path rather than this one.

Two honest notes. First, this does shrink your reach, because accounts under 18 stop seeing you entirely. That is the cost of operating legally and it is not optional. Second, "18+" in the bio is decoration, not a control. The account setting is the control.

Do the same on Facebook while you are in there: the Page's settings carry an age restriction option, set it to 18+. And do not boost anything on Meta until you have checked alcohol ad eligibility for South Africa in Ads Manager, because the rules differ by country and change without notice. Organic posting with an age gate is fine. Paid is a separate approval and a separate fight.

## 2. Content pillars

Four pillars, taken from the brand's own world rather than a marketing template. Each one has a job, and every post on the calendar belongs to exactly one.

### The Liquid
The bottle, the can, the pour, the numbers. Three years in oak. French oak to finish. 43%. 750ml. R450.

**What it is for:** making someone want the drink, and carrying the facts that make the price make sense. This is the pillar that sells. It is also the one with the most existing photography, which is why it carries the heaviest load in month one.

### VIR DIÉ WAT WEET
Knowledge. How to serve it, what oak actually does, what people get wrong about brandy, what to eat alongside it.

**What it is for:** authority, and saves. Saves and shares carry more weight than likes, as far as anyone outside Instagram can tell, and a useful carousel gets kept where a bottle photo gets a tap and forgotten. It also does the work of the tagline without ever repeating it. The brand says it is for those who know, so hand people something to know.

### Where It's Found
The rooms. Bars, restaurants, markets, events, and the box arriving at somebody's door.

**What it is for:** proof. A new brand's biggest problem is not being disliked, it is being unproven. Every event photograph is evidence that this is a real thing in real places. It is also your stockist pipeline, because bar owners see other bars.

### The House
Silverton. 2020. Two founders. The rule that nothing leaves unless it earns the label.

**What it is for:** the reason to care about this brandy over the one next to it on the shelf. Use it sparingly. A house that explains itself constantly is a house that is nervous. Roughly one post in four, and never two in a row.

## 4. Caption rules

The rules for writing more of these yourself, after the month runs out.

### Openers that work

The first line is the only line most people read, because Instagram cuts the caption after roughly 125 characters. Get the whole idea into the first eight words.

- **A fact with no adjective.** "Nose: warm oak, dried apricot, vanilla."
- **A flat statement of place.** "Silverton, Pretoria."
- **A number.** "Two bottles, R850."
- **A small correction.** "Four things people get wrong about brandy."
- **A line from the canon, used once.** "Some rules are meant to be questioned."
- **A time, stated plainly.** "Friday, properly."

The pattern underneath all six: the opener states something, it does not ask for something. Quiet is not vagueness. Quiet is knowing the fact is enough.

### Never do this

- **Never the maker vocabulary.** The words the whole spirits category leans on to sound small, hand-made and workshop-scale are all out, in captions, in hashtags and in alt text. The full list is in the brand voice rules, so keep it open while you write. If a word makes the house sound like a hobby, it does not go in. Name the process instead. Three years in oak does more work than any of them.
- **Never defend the business.** No licence talk, no explaining that you are allowed to exist, no answering an accusation nobody made. Defensiveness reads as guilt.
- **Never these characters:** em dashes, en dashes, ellipsis characters, middots. Commas and full stops do everything you need. If a sentence needs a dash to hold together, it needs to be two sentences.
- **Never number a batch or call anything limited.** The products are permanent. Scarcity theatre is the opposite of quiet confidence.
- **Never stack superlatives.** "Finest premium ultra smooth" says nothing. One specific number beats three adjectives every time.
- **Never these openers:** "We are so excited to announce", "Happy Friday everyone", "Who else loves brandy?", "Tag a friend who", "Double tap if", "Link in bio!!!"
- **Never tie the drink to driving, sport, work performance, coping with anything, or sexual or social success.** This is a legal line, not a stylistic one.
- **Never show, imply, or address anyone under 18.** No school references, no youth-coded slang. In practice that means nobody under 25 in front of the camera, because the camera flattens age and the platform will not give you the benefit of the doubt.
- **Emoji:** one, rarely, and never as a bullet point. A brand that whispers does not decorate.

### Structure that repeats

```
Opening line. Short. Under eight words.

The middle. Two or three sentences that carry
one fact and one image. Never a list unless it
is a carousel.

The ask, if there is one. One line. A domain
name or an email address, stated flat.

Drink responsibly. Not for sale to persons under 18.

Three to five hashtags.
```

Vary the length deliberately. A month of identical caption shapes reads as automated. Day 20 is two sentences. Day 24 is four paragraphs. Both are correct.

One CTA per post, maximum, and only about one post in three should carry one at all. Name the domain rather than saying "link in bio". A brand that expects you to find it is more convincing than one that directs traffic.

The sign-off is fixed. "Drink responsibly. Not for sale to persons under 18." goes on every feed post. It stops being copy after a week and becomes furniture, which is exactly what a compliance line should be.

### Hashtag strategy for South African reach

**What may have changed, and why it matters either way.** Instagram has been widely reported to cap hashtags at five per post and reel, with the cap counted across caption and comments together, which would end the old trick of hiding thirty tags in the first comment. Treat that as reported, not settled: try to post a sixth tag once and see what the app does, and check the current rule in the Instagram Help Centre before you build a system on it. Separately, Adam Mosseri has said publicly that hashtags do not drive reach. What they still do is label the topic so the recommendation system knows what the post is about and can surface it in search.

**So the strategy is:** three to five tags, all specific, none decorative. That sits inside the cap whether the cap is real or not. Then put the actual work into the things that do drive distribution.

**What replaced hashtags for a zero-traffic account:**

1. **Keywords in the caption.** Instagram search reads caption text. "South African brandy" written in a sentence does more than the hashtag version.
2. **Alt text on every post.** Settings → Accessibility → Write alt text, before you publish. Describe the image plainly and let the keywords fall in naturally. Almost nobody does this, which is why it is worth doing.
3. **The location tag.** Tag Silverton or Pretoria on every single post. For a brand with no audience yet, a local tag is worth more than any national hashtag.
4. **Saves and shares.** The carousels and the knowledge posts exist because those two signals appear to outrank likes. That is why the pillars are weighted the way they are.

**The pool.** Pick three to five per post from this list. Keep #VerbotenSpirits on everything so you build a searchable archive of your own.

- Always: `#VerbotenSpirits`
- Category: `#SouthAfricanBrandy` `#BrandySA` `#Brandy` `#SASpirits`
- Place: `#Pretoria` `#Silverton` `#Gauteng`
- Occasion or topic: `#HomeBar` `#FoodPairing` `#TastingNotes` `#FridayPour` `#BrandyAndCola` `#FrenchOak` `#Biltong`

**Honest limitation:** I have not verified live post volumes or competition for these specific tags, so treat the list as chosen for relevance rather than sized for reach. Given the tag cap and the reach reality, sizing them would not change much anyway. Spend the hour on alt text instead.

Two things to avoid in the pool. Any tag built on the maker vocabulary, which rules out most of the high-volume spirits tags. And generic engagement tags like `#InstaGood` or `#DrinkStagram`, which now signal spam to the very system you are trying to please.

## 5. When there is nothing to post

Three formats that need no news, no event, and no new photography. Any of them can be turned into a post in under twenty minutes.

### 1. The same bottle, a different light

One product shot you already own, plus one fact you have not said yet. That is the whole format. The bottle never gets boring as long as the sentence next to it is new.

A fact bank to draw from, one per post:

- Three years in oak, then French oak casks to finish. Two stages, one bottle.
- 43%. 750ml. R450. Three numbers, no adjectives.
- French oak has a tighter grain than American oak, so it gives up what it holds more slowly.
- 750ml is fifteen 50ml pours.
- R450 across fifteen pours is thirty rand a glass. Compare that to the last cocktail you paid for.
- We pour it into a tumbler, not a snifter. More air, and the glass sits still.
- The finish is the part that tells you whether the money was well spent. Ours is long and smooth.
- Nose first: warm oak, dried apricot, vanilla. Give it a minute before the first sip.
- Palate: caramel, toasted nuts, then a subtle spice underneath.
- Room temperature is a setting, not a rule. One clear cube changes the whole reading.
- Silverton, Pretoria. Nobody thinks of it as brandy country.
- Delivery is R150 anywhere in South Africa, free from R2500, dispatch in one to two weeks.

### 2. One line on black

A single sentence from the house canon, set in the brand typeface, white on black, logo small in a corner. No photograph. No caption beyond the same line, or no caption at all. Post it and walk away.

Lines you own and can use:

- Some rules are meant to be questioned.
- Nothing leaves unless it earns the label.
- VIR DIÉ WAT WEET
- MEMORIES NOT REGRETS
- Pure Spirit. Pure Mischief.
- A quiet rebellion in a glass.
- Some traditions are meant to be whispered, not shouted.
- Verboten. German for forbidden.

Use one of these no more than twice a month. Their power is entirely in how rarely they appear. A line you post weekly is a slogan, and slogans are the thing this brand is supposed to be the opposite of.

### 3. Answer one question in public

Take a real question, from a DM, a market stall, or a friend who does not drink brandy, and answer it properly. Three carousel slides or a twenty second reel with the bottle in frame and your voice over it. No face needed.

Seed questions when the real ones run dry:

- What is the difference between brandy and cognac?
- Can I put ice in good brandy?
- What do I serve it in if I do not own proper glasses?
- Is South African brandy actually any good, honestly?
- What do I bring to someone's house if they already have everything?
- Why does yours cost R450 when the shelf next to it starts lower?

That last one is the single most valuable post you can make, and most brands are too nervous to make it. Answer it with numbers. Three years in oak, a French oak finish, 43%, fifteen pours, thirty rand a glass. No adjectives. Let the arithmetic do the arguing.

## 6. Your actual voice, and how it differs from this pack

Added after reading the real Facebook posts from April to July 2026. Some of what is written above assumed a brand that posts in English and treats Afrikaans as a rare flourish. That is not what you do, and the pack is the thing that was wrong.

### What the real posts actually do

Reading the Lavender Kontrei Mark posts back, the pattern is consistent and it works:

- **You switch language wholesale, not word by word.** A post is either English or Afrikaans. "Die manne is reg, die stalletjie staan sterk, en die brandewyn is YSKOUD" is a whole post, not a line dropped into English copy.
- **You invite rather than announce.** "Kom maak 'n draai", "Kom sê hallo", "Sien julle daar". The reader is being asked over, not sold to.
- **Every post carries the practical block.** Date, time, place, directions past Obaro, entry fee. Nobody has to ask a follow-up question.
- **You hang posts on the occasion, not the product.** Mother's Day, the Bokke on the big screen, the XC championships, Dirk van der Westhuizen live at 11:00. The brandy is what is in your hand while the occasion happens.
- **The compliance line is always there**, in the language of the post.

That is a good voice. It is warmer than the website, and that is correct: the website is the shop window, the feed is the stall. They should not sound identical.

### The bilingual policy

Write each post in one language. Do not mix.

- **Afrikaans for market and event posts.** This is your home crowd, at a Pretoria kontrei mark, and the Afrikaans posts read noticeably better than the English ones.
- **English for product, story and shipping posts.** These reach people who do not know the brand and may not be in Gauteng.
- **Roughly half and half over a month.** That is close to what you already do.

When a market post matters enough to reach both, write the Afrikaans version as the caption and put two English lines at the end. Never a full duplicate translation, it reads like a form.

### Afrikaans compliance line

Use the one you already use, it is correct:

```
Waarskuwing: Alkohol kan skadelik wees vir jou gesondheid. Nie vir persone onder 18 nie.
```

English posts keep: `Not for sale to persons under the age of 18. Drink responsibly.`

### What to change from what you are already doing

Four things, all small:

1. **Use "craft" only when it is literally true.** You do make special small runs for some events, and calling those craft gins is accurate, so keep it there. What the brand brief bans is craft as an identity, the generic badge every South African spirits brand wears. So: "a craft gin made for the day" is fine, #CraftDistillery as a permanent tag is not. Default to saying what it actually is: "our gins", "distilled in Pretoria", "made for this market only".
2. **Cut the emoji count by about half.** Two or three in a post reads warm. Eight reads like a flyer. Keep them where they replace a bullet, drop them where they decorate.
3. **Name the drink properly and consistently.** The posts variously say Brandy & Cola, Brandy & Coke, and Premium Brandy & Cola. Pick one written form and hold it.
4. **Add one line of destination.** Almost every post ends at the stall. One in three should end at the site or the bar booking, because the market crowd is the warmest audience you will ever have and they are currently walking away with nothing but a cup.

### What not to change

The invitation, the practical block, the Afrikaans, the occasion-first framing, and "Pure Mischief". Those are working. The 30 day calendar above should be read through this section, not instead of it: keep the structure and the pillars, and let roughly half of it be written in Afrikaans in the register you already use.
