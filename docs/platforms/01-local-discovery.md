# Verboten Local Discovery Pack

Everything below is finished and ready to paste. Where a platform rule could bite you, I have said so plainly instead of guessing.

**Total time: about 4 hours across the first month.** Most of it is in week one.

**One honest caveat up front.** Google Business Profile is usually the highest return item for a local business. For an alcohol producer with no public tasting room it might not be available at all. Section 1.1 tells you how to find out in ten minutes. Sections 2 to 5 apply either way, so do those regardless.

## 0. Do this first (10 minutes)

**Pick one Google account and never use another one for the business.** Ideally a Google account on `info@verboten.co.za`, not a personal Gmail. Everything in this pack (Business Profile, Search Console, later Bing) hangs off that one login. If you lose access to it later you lose the lot.

**Lock the NAP block.** NAP is name, address, phone. Google matches your business across the web by comparing these three strings. They must be identical everywhere, down to the punctuation. Use this exact block on the site, on the Business Profile, in your email signature and in every directory:

```
Verboten Spirits
Silverton, Pretoria, Gauteng, 0081
South Africa
+27 75 387 3456
info@verboten.co.za
https://verboten.co.za
```

Never write it as "Verboten Pty Ltd" or "+27 (0) 75 387 3456" or "0753873456" in a listing. One format, everywhere.

**A note on the page paths in this pack.** The domain is certain. The paths are not mine to promise. Wherever you see something like `/shop` or `/story` below, open your own sitemap first and paste the real URL. Section 2.3 shows you where the sitemap lives.

## 1. Google Business Profile

### 1.1 Are you actually eligible? Read this before you create anything.

Google's rule is that a profile represents a business that makes **in person contact with customers**, either at a place customers can visit, or at a place you travel out from to reach them. A business that only takes orders online and ships by courier is **not eligible**, and profiles created anyway get suspended. Suspension is a pain to reverse.

So there are three honest routes. Pick the one that is true.

**Route A. Customers can come to Silverton, even if only by appointment.**
Collections, arranged tastings, trade buyers picking up stock. This qualifies as a normal listing with a **visible address**. Set the "Appointment required" attribute and hours accordingly. This is the strongest option, because a visible address is what gets you into Google Maps results for searches like "brandy Pretoria".

**Route B. No visitors at Silverton, but you personally deliver in Gauteng.**
Your own car, your own hands, to customers or to the bars that stock you. That is a **service area business**: address hidden, service areas listed. Set service areas to Pretoria and Johannesburg, not "South Africa". Google's guidance is to list only areas you can realistically reach, and there is a cap on how many you may add. Check the current number on their guidelines page rather than take mine. Claiming a whole country from one location is the kind of thing that gets a profile suspended.

**Route C. Everything ships by courier and nobody ever meets you.**
Then you are not eligible today, and you should not force it. Skip to section 2, and revisit the Business Profile the day you open a tasting room, take a stall at a market, or start doing your own Gauteng runs. Any of those three make Route A or B honest.

**Check this yourself rather than take my word for it:** in Google's help centre, read "Guidelines for representing your business on Google". The eligibility section is near the top. Ten minutes. If your situation is not obviously covered there, do not create the profile.

**On verification, be realistic.** Verification methods vary by category, country and account, and video verification is common now: you record an unbroken walkthrough showing the premises from the street, evidence it is yours (signage, stock, production equipment, tools, keys), and something with your name on it such as a utility bill, invoice book or your liquor licence. An unmarked unit with no signage is where most producers fail. Get a sign on the door before you record. I cannot tell you which method Google will offer you, so have the licence and a utility bill ready either way.

**On alcohol specifically:** Google does not ban alcohol businesses from having a profile. Distilleries, bottle stores and wine producers list normally. The restrictions bite on **paid ads** and on **promotional post content**, which I cover in 1.9. If anything on Google's eligibility page contradicts what I have written here, the page wins. It changes more often than I do.

### 1.2 Business name

Enter exactly this and nothing else:

```
Verboten Spirits
```

Not "Verboten Spirits | Premium South African Brandy". Not "Verboten Spirits Pretoria". Adding keywords or a location to the name field is against Google's name policy. The registered entity name, Verboten Pty Ltd, goes on invoices, not on the profile.

### 1.3 Categories

The category picker is a fixed list that Google controls, it differs by country, and it changes. So here is the method rather than a promise.

**Primary category.** Type "distillery" into the field. **Distillery** has been a Google category for years, so it is very likely there. If you distil, or if you mature, blend and bottle under your own label, this is the closest accurate description available, and it is the one that puts you in Maps for spirits searches.

Only if Distillery does not appear, try in this order: **Liquor wholesaler**, **Beverage distributor**, **Manufacturer**.

**Secondary categories.** Add at most two, and only if they are true:

- **Liquor store**, only under Route A, and only if a person can actually buy a bottle from you on site. This category promises a shop. If there is no shop, leave it out.
- **Beverage distributor** or **Liquor wholesaler**, if you sell to bars and restaurants directly.

**What I am not certain about, and how to settle it:** I cannot confirm which of "Liquor wholesaler", "Beverage distributor" and "Alcohol manufacturer" are live in the South African category list this month. The picker is the truth. Type each word, take what appears, and apply one rule with no exceptions: **never pick a category that promises a service you do not offer.** Your primary category drives most of what you rank for, so get that one right and treat the rest as decoration.

### 1.4 Address

**Route A:** enter the full street address in Silverton and leave it visible. Then drag the map pin onto the actual door, not the middle of the street. Google places the pin automatically and it is often out by a fair distance in industrial areas.

**Route B:** enter the full address during setup, because Google needs it to verify you, then answer "no" to "do you serve customers at your business address". The address is then hidden and only the service areas show publicly. Google still holds the address on file, it is just not on display.

**If Silverton is a residential address, use Route B.** Publishing a home address on Maps invites people to your gate at 22:00 on a Saturday.

### 1.5 Hours

Set hours that describe when a human answers `+27 75 387 3456`. Nothing more.

```
Monday to Friday    09:00 to 17:00
Saturday            Closed
Sunday              Closed
```

Under Route A, also tick the **Appointment required** attribute, so nobody drives to Silverton expecting a shop floor. Under Route B, hours describe when you answer and dispatch.

Do not publish hours that imply walk-in access you do not offer. "Closed when it said open" is the fastest route to a one star review.

Add **special hours** for South African public holidays as they come up. Google shows an "hours might differ" warning on holidays, and a profile that answers the question looks maintained.

### 1.6 Attributes to tick

Attributes vary by category and country. Tick only what is true, and expect the list you actually see to differ slightly from this one.

Tick:

- **Delivery** (you deliver nationally)
- **Online appointments** or **Appointment required**, under Route A
- **Wheelchair accessible entrance**, under Route A, if true
- **In store pickup** or **Kerbside pickup**, only if collection is genuinely available
- Payments: **Credit cards**, **Debit cards**. PayFast handles cards and Instant EFT, so both are accurate for online orders.

Leave off:

- **In store shopping**, unless there is a real shop
- **Same day delivery**. Dispatch is one to two weeks. Do not promise otherwise.
- Anything about dining, seating or drinking on site. That is a licence question, not a marketing one.

**Links to fill in:**

- Website: `https://verboten.co.za`
- Order online, or shop link: your shop page URL, taken from the sitemap

WhatsApp is not a native Business Profile field. Some accounts get a messaging feature, many in South Africa do not. If it is not offered to you, leave it, the number is on the site.

### 1.7 Business description (paste as is)

```
Verboten is an independent brandy house in Silverton, Pretoria. Two founders started it in 2020, quietly, while most of the world was busy following orders.

Verboten Premium Brandy spends three years in oak and is finished in French oak casks. 43% ABV, 750ml. Warm oak, dried apricot and vanilla on the nose. Caramel, toasted nuts and a little spice on the palate. A long, smooth finish. Also from us: the two bottle Premium Set, and Verboten Brandy and Cola in a 440ml can.

Order online for delivery anywhere in South Africa, free on larger orders. You will also find us in bars, restaurants and at events that care what they pour.

Pure Spirit. Pure Mischief.

Drink responsibly. Not for sale to persons under 18.
```

That sits comfortably inside Google's description limit. The field shows you the current limit and counts as you paste, so trust the counter, not me.

Three deliberate choices in there:

- **No URLs.** Google's rules for this field disallow links.
- **No rand figures.** The same rules disallow prices and promotional content. "Free on larger orders" is a fact about your service. "R150 flat, free over R2500" reads as an offer and could get the description rejected. The exact figures live on the site and in Posts, where they are fine.
- **The 18+ line stays.** It is the correct posture for an alcohol brand on a public surface.

### 1.8 Photos, in priority order

Profiles with photos give people something to look at and Google something to show. You already have the assets.

1. **Logo.** Square, and the largest clean version you have. The upload panel states Google's current size guidance.
2. **Cover.** A studio bottle shot on black. This is the image most people see first.
3. **Product photos.** Bottle, can, two bottle set. One each, minimum.
4. **Event photographs.** Three to five of the real ones. They are worth more than they look, because they are the only proof on the profile that other people drink this.
5. **Exterior**, Route A only, showing the signage. It also helps the video verification story hang together.

Add two or three photos a month after that. A profile that never changes looks abandoned, to Google and to people.

### 1.9 Google Posts

**Read this before posting.** Business Profile content is governed by a content policy that restricts regulated goods, and alcohol sits in that group. In practice, distilleries and bottle stores post regularly without trouble, but I cannot promise your posts will never be removed. Reduce the risk this way:

- Use the **"Learn more"** button, pointing at a product page. Avoid "Buy" style calls to action.
- Keep prices and discount language out of Posts. Describe the product, link to the page, let the page sell.
- Never show or imply anyone under 25 drinking. Never tie the drink to driving, sport, work performance, courage or coping.
- Put the responsibility line at the end of every post.

If a post is removed, edit it and repost. Find the Business Profile content policy in Google's help centre and read it before your first post, so you know what you agreed to.

Post roughly every two to four weeks. Older posts drop down the panel, so a slow steady drip beats a burst.

## 2. Google Search Console

The best return per minute in this whole document. It is the only place Google tells you directly what it thinks of your site.

### 2.1 Add the property (5 minutes)

1. Go to `search.google.com/search-console` and sign in with the business Google account from section 0.
2. If this is your first property you land straight on the picker. Otherwise click the property dropdown top left, then **Add property**.
3. Choose the **Domain** option on the left, not URL prefix. Domain covers `verboten.co.za`, `www.verboten.co.za`, http, https and every subdomain in one property. URL prefix would need four separate properties to do the same job.
4. Type `verboten.co.za`. No `https://`, no `www`, no trailing slash.
5. Click **Continue**. Google shows a TXT record. It looks like `google-site-verification=` followed by a long string. Click **Copy**.
6. Leave this tab open. Do not click Verify yet.

If Search Console detects Cloudflare and offers to add the record for you after you sign in to Cloudflare, you can take that shortcut and skip to 2.3. It appears for some providers and accounts and not others. If you do not see it, do it manually below, which works in every case.

### 2.2 Add the TXT record in Cloudflare (3 minutes)

1. Go to `dash.cloudflare.com` and sign in.
2. Click **verboten.co.za** in the site list.
3. Left sidebar: **DNS**, then **Records**.
4. Click **Add record**.
5. **Type**: choose `TXT`.
6. **Name**: type `@`. Cloudflare will display it as `verboten.co.za`. That is correct. Do not type the domain into this field yourself, and do not type `www`.
7. **Content**: paste the whole string, including the `google-site-verification=` part. Nothing else in the field, no quotation marks.
8. **TTL**: leave on `Auto`.
9. There is no proxy toggle on TXT records. If you are hunting for the orange cloud, it is not there, and that is normal.
10. Click **Save**.

This record changes nothing about how the site serves. TXT records are notes, not routing.

### 2.3 Verify and submit the sitemap (3 minutes)

1. Back in the Search Console tab, click **Verify**. Cloudflare usually publishes the record fast, so it often passes first time. If it fails, wait five minutes and click Verify again. Google allows time for DNS to propagate, so a slow pass is not a failed one.
2. **Leave the TXT record in place permanently.** Deleting it later un-verifies the property and you silently lose access.
3. Before submitting anything, open `https://verboten.co.za/sitemap.xml` in a browser. You should see a list of URLs covering the home page, the shop, the story page, the find us page, the contact page, the legal pages and each product page. Write those exact paths down, because they are the ones you paste into Posts and links elsewhere in this pack. If the sitemap errors or 404s, stop and get it fixed before you submit. Submitting a broken sitemap teaches Google nothing and wastes a week.
4. In Search Console, left sidebar: **Indexing**, then **Sitemaps**.
5. In the "Add a new sitemap" box, type `sitemap.xml`. If the field already shows the domain in front of the box, that is all you type. If it asks for a full URL, paste `https://verboten.co.za/sitemap.xml`.
6. Click **Submit**. Status should read **Success** within a few minutes to a day. "Couldn't fetch" on the first check is common and usually clears itself, so do not resubmit five times.
7. Submit it once. Once is enough, forever. The sitemap regenerates itself and Google re-reads it on its own schedule.

While you are there, open `https://verboten.co.za/robots.txt`. If it declares the sitemap too, Google has two ways to find it. If it does not, ask whoever maintains the site to add the line.

### 2.4 Then do this, once (10 minutes)

- **URL Inspection**, the search bar at the top: paste your home page URL, then click **Request indexing**. Repeat for the shop page, the story page and the three product URLs. Six URLs, about a minute each. This is the only time manual indexing requests are worth doing. Do not make a habit of it.
- **Settings**, then **Users and permissions**: confirm only your account is listed.
- Check **Security and Manual actions** in the left sidebar. Both should say no issues. If either does not, that is the only genuine emergency in this entire document.

### 2.5 What to look at in month one

Check it **once a week**, for five minutes, on a Monday. Not daily.

1. **Indexing, then Pages.** The number that matters early: how many pages are indexed. You want the home page, the shop page, the story page, the find us page, the contact page and the three product pages in the indexed list within two to three weeks. If the home page is still not indexed after 21 days, something is wrong and that is worth chasing.
2. **Performance, then the Queries tab.** Not clicks. **Impressions**, and which words trigger them. Early on it will be almost entirely "verboten", "verboten brandy", "verboten spirits". That is brand search. It proves indexing works, but it is not growth. The moment a non brand query like "south african brandy" or "brandy pretoria" appears, even at position 60, that is your first real signal.
3. **Sitemaps.** Discovered URL count should roughly match what is in the file.

### 2.6 What NOT to panic about

- **"Crawled, currently not indexed"** and **"Discovered, currently not indexed"**. Both are normal on a new site with nothing linking to it. They mean "we know about it, we are not convinced yet". Give it weeks, not days.
- **Zero clicks for the first month.** Expected. New domain, no authority. Clicks follow impressions, impressions follow indexing, indexing takes weeks.
- **Excluded pages.** Cart, checkout, account and admin URLs, plus redirect URLs, will show as excluded or blocked by robots. That is normal and intended on a shop.
- **Wild average position numbers.** Position 4.2 one week and 61 the next, off nine impressions, is noise. Position means nothing until impressions run into the hundreds.
- **Core Web Vitals saying "not enough data".** It needs real traffic before it can report anything. This will say nothing useful for months.
- **Any single day showing zero.** Search Console data lags a couple of days and weekends are quieter. Read the 28 day trend, never the daily bars.
- **404s from old URLs.** This is the one item here that does need action. If an earlier version of the site used different product URLs, Google may still have them. Watch the **Not found (404)** list in the Pages report during weeks two to four. If old product URLs appear, add 301 redirects to the matching current product page. That recovers whatever ranking the old pages held, instead of throwing it away.

## 3. Analytics

### 3.1 Use Cloudflare Web Analytics. Here is why.

It sets **no cookies** and does no cross site tracking, so it keeps you out of the consent banner question, and you do not have to solve POPIA consent before you can see a single number. It is free. Check Cloudflare's current terms and limits when you sign up, since those are theirs to change.

One thing to confirm before you count on the easy path: the site's Content Security Policy has to allow the beacon, which means `static.cloudflareinsights.com` in `script-src` and `cloudflareinsights.com` in `connect-src`. Ask whoever maintains the site to check. If it is already allowed, nothing needs rebuilding or redeploying.

For a new site run by one person, that combination beats a better tool you have to configure, legally document and consent-gate before it tells you anything.

### 3.2 Click path (5 minutes)

1. `dash.cloudflare.com`, sign in.
2. Left sidebar: **Analytics and Logs**, then **Web Analytics**. On some account layouts it sits under the site itself rather than at account level. Either entry point reaches the same tool.
3. Click **Add a site**.
4. Enter `verboten.co.za`.
5. **If the site's DNS is proxied through Cloudflare** (the record shows an orange cloud, meaning traffic passes through Cloudflare), choose the **automatic setup**. Cloudflare injects the beacon at the edge. No code change, no deploy, done.
6. **If DNS is set to "DNS only"** (grey cloud), Cloudflare gives you a small JavaScript snippet instead. That has to be pasted into the site's layout and deployed. It is a ten minute developer job, not a founder job. Tell whoever maintains the site: "add the Cloudflare Web Analytics beacon to the root layout".
7. Wait an hour, then load `https://verboten.co.za` on your phone with wifi off, and confirm the visit appears.

**Add one line to the privacy policy** either way, because being able to point at it costs nothing:

```
We use Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits to this site. It does not use cookies, it does not track you across other websites, and it does not build a profile of you.
```

I am not a lawyer and that is not legal advice. It is an accurate description of what the tool does.

### 3.3 GA4, honestly

GA4 is more powerful. It does full ecommerce funnels, revenue by traffic source, and it tells you which channel actually produced an order. Cloudflare Web Analytics cannot do any of that.

What that power costs you:

- It sets cookies and sends data to Google, which under POPIA puts you in consent territory. Doing it properly means a cookie banner, a consent mode setup, and a privacy policy that describes it accurately.
- The banner itself costs conversions and becomes another thing to maintain.
- Configuring GA4 ecommerce events on a custom checkout with PayFast is real developer work, not a checkbox.
- If the site's CSP already allows `googletagmanager.com` and `google-analytics.com`, the technical door is open. Confirm that rather than assume it. Either way, consent is the hard part, not the tag.

**My call: skip GA4 now.** At close to zero traffic it would tell you nothing that Cloudflare plus your order list does not. Revisit it when traffic is steady enough that the channel question actually has an answer, or the day you spend money on traffic and need to know which rand produced which order. Do not run both. That is two consent problems and one answer.

### 3.4 The three numbers to actually watch

Weekly, five minutes. Write them in a spreadsheet, one row per week. The trend is the only part that means anything.

1. **Visits per week.** Cloudflare Web Analytics, Visits. Not the absolute number, the direction over four weeks. This tells you whether what you did that week reached anybody.
2. **Product page views per week.** Cloudflare, Top pages, add up the shop page and the three product pages. This separates "people found the brand" from "people considered buying". If visits climb but product views do not, your home page is the leak.
3. **Orders per week.** Your own admin, not analytics. The only number that pays for anything.

Then one ratio, worked out by hand: **product page views divided by orders**. At low volume that is the only conversion rate worth trusting, because it is built from two numbers you can count on your fingers instead of an analytics estimate. There is no published benchmark worth quoting at your volume, so compare it against itself. Log it for a few months. If the ratio holds steady while traffic grows, the site is doing its job and your problem is traffic. If it gets worse as traffic grows, the product page is the leak, and more traffic will not fix that.

A fourth number lives in Search Console rather than analytics: **total impressions per month**. Watch it for direction only.

## 4. Bing

### 4.1 Bing Webmaster Tools (10 minutes, do it once)

Bing is a small slice of search here. It also feeds some AI answer tools, which is a growing way people find brands, so ten minutes now is cheap.

1. Go to `bing.com/webmasters`.
2. **Sign in with Google**, using the same business Google account. This matters. The import only works if the accounts line up.
3. Choose **Import your sites from Google Search Console**.
4. Click **Continue**, then allow access when Google asks.
5. Select `verboten.co.za` from the list, click **Import**.
6. Verification and the sitemap should carry across. Confirm under **Sitemaps** that `sitemap.xml` is listed, and add it by hand if it is not.

Do not attempt this until Search Console verification has passed, or there is nothing to import.

Then leave it alone. Check it once a quarter. If it flags a crawl error that Search Console does not, believe Search Console.

**Optional one switch extra:** in Cloudflare, under **Caching**, there is a **Crawler Hints** setting. Turning it on tells Bing and a few other engines when your pages change, so they crawl fresh content sooner and waste less of your bandwidth. Free, one switch, modest but real upside.

### 4.2 Bing Places, honestly

**Worth it only once the Google profile is verified, and only then.** Bing Places has offered an import flow that copies a Google Business Profile across in a few clicks, which would make it a fifteen minute job rather than a rebuild. Check whether that path is still offered when you get there. If it is not, do not build the listing by hand. The traffic does not justify the typing.

The same eligibility logic applies, and Bing's own rules on regulated categories are something to check on their eligibility page rather than assume. Put this at the bottom of month one and drop it without guilt if the month runs out.

## Appendix A. The one thing that beats everything else in this pack

**Reviews.** They are one of the few local ranking inputs you control directly, and a profile with real reviews does better than one with none, in Maps and in the local pack. You already have people who bought bottles and people who tasted at events.

Never offer anything in exchange for a review. That is against Google's policy, and it is grounds for removing the reviews and acting against the profile. Just ask, personally, once, and only people who genuinely liked it.

Reply to every single one, good or bad, in the house voice. Short. No defensiveness. A one line reply on a five star review is worth writing.

## Appendix B. Two small technical items

**1. Add contact details to the Organization schema.** The site outputs Organization structured data. Ask your developer to open the SEO helper in the project, `src/lib/seo.ts`, and check the object that holds name, address and social links. If it carries no phone or email, adding `telephone: "+27753873456"` and `email: "info@verboten.co.za"` gives Google a machine readable version of your NAP that matches the Business Profile exactly. Five minutes of developer time.

**2. Check the old product URLs.** If an earlier version of the site used different product slugs, Google may still hold them, and they need 301 redirects to the matching current product pages. Search Console's 404 report in week three will tell you whether this is a real problem or a non issue. Do not guess at it now, wait for the report.
